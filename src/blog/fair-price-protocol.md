Here's something that feels unfair because it is a little unfair: you and your neighbour can buy the very same thing and pay different amounts. Same product, same day, different price. It isn't magic and it isn't always a mistake. It's how a lot of modern pricing works.

This article is the short, practical version. For the full explanation, read [why two people pay different prices for the same thing]({{BASE}}blog/personalized-pricing/) and [what the loyalty penalty is and how to beat it]({{BASE}}blog/loyalty-penalty/).

## Pricing isn't one fixed number anymore

For many things we buy — insurance, internet, phone plans, travel, subscriptions — the price isn't carved in stone. It moves. It can move with demand, with the time of day, and sometimes with what a seller guesses about *you*: the device you're on, where you are, how you've behaved before, how likely they think you are to just say yes.

When a price is shaped around a person or a moment rather than a fixed list, small differences add up. Two people, two slightly different prices, and neither of them sees the other's screen.

## The loyalty penalty

The strangest part is that staying put can cost you. In a lot of markets, the best offers are pointed at *new* customers — the people a company is trying to win. The people who already signed up and never looked again? Their price often drifts quietly upward, renewal after renewal.

So loyalty, the thing we think of as a virtue, can turn into a penalty. Not because anyone sent you a nasty letter, but because nothing pushed back. The default is to let it ride, and the default usually favours the seller.

## You don't have to beat the system, just check it

The good news: you don't need to become a full-time deal hunter. You just need a habit that interrupts the drift. A few minutes of checking, done on purpose, cancels out most of the quiet creep.

Here are the six moves of the **Fair Price Protocol**:

1. **Set your number before you open the app.** Decide the most you'll pay before you browse, and write it down. Never buy straight from a push notification.
2. **Do the second-screen check.** For bigger purchases, look at the same item on a second device, logged out. A different price is your cue to ask the seller or buy elsewhere.
3. **Trust the price history, not the sale badge.** "40% off" means little until you know what it cost before.
4. **Hold a Switching Day, once a year.** List your contracts, get one new-customer price for each big one, then ask: "Can you match this, or should I switch?"
5. **Give your AI shopper rules.** A hard budget it never reveals, at least three offers, and it asks you before any payment.
6. **Look for the label.** Where the rules require shops to say a price was personalised, read the small print, and report it if it's missing.

That's it. No spreadsheets-of-doom, no confrontation, no gurus.
